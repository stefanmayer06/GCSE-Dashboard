Volta is the proposed name for the product now called GCSE Study Desk. The mark is built from the app's own quadrant shapes: three quarters for the three courses, and a fourth in volt for the mastery you earn. The circle only closes when you fix what you missed.

The name

One word, four meanings, all already in the app

Most names in this market describe the product ("Save My Exams") or bolt on a prefix ("Revvo", "Revu"). Volta tells the product's story in a word a 15-year-old can say, spell and use as a verb.

Volt

Volt lime is already the app's one reward colour: the go button, earned stars and mastered tiles. The name makes that colour the brand.

The pile

In 1800 Alessandro Volta made the first battery by stacking discs. Circuit emblems also stack layers, and each layer comes from marked answers.

The turn

In a sonnet the volta is the turning point, where the argument changes direction. GCSE English students learn the term, and a fixed mistake is the same kind of turning point.

Una volta

In Italian, ancora una volta means "one more time". Mistakes come back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days, and each return is one more go.

The mark

The Volta disc

The mark reuses the Shapez quadrant grammar behind every topic emblem, so the logo and the app use the same shapes. It keeps the favicon you already ship and adds one idea: the volt quarter is still growing.

  3. Maths HigherPurple, bottom left.
  4. The volt quarterMastery. It is drawn at 62% size, a piece still being built. The gap it leaves is the mistake you haven't fixed yet.

Wordmark and lockups

Lower case, heavy, tight

The wordmark is set in Unbounded ExtraBold, the app's display face, with tighter spacing and converted to outlines. Lower case keeps it friendly, in the tradition of duolingo, mimo and intel.

What that means for the brand

Stay away from the bolt

Save My Exams' logo is a lightning bolt and Seneca's hero uses ⚡. Volta says electricity in its name, so the mark uses the quadrant disc instead.

Sell the loop, not the bank

Free papers and questions are everywhere (Maths Genie, Corbettmaths, PMT, Bitesize). The only story here that competitors can't copy by adding content is the mistake-to-mastery loop, and the closing disc shows it.

Be the game, not the library

Seneca, Save My Exams and Medly all open on white with flat blue or black, and none of them leads with a character. Their gamification is a feature, not the brand. Volta can make it the brand, with Pip and the night stage.

Names considered

VoltaRecommended

Four meanings that match the product and easy to say. No GCSE or UK revision brand found with the name, though a tutoring-management app and a US academy use it. Plain "Volta" is taken on the App Store by a college wellbeing app, so list as "Volta: GCSE Revision".

QuadoRunner-up

"Quad" plus "o", like Lego and Mimo, for the four quarters. Cleaner trademark space (only Quadoo, a word game, and Kuado nearby) but a thinner story. Use it if Volta fails the trade-mark search.

PipitRejected

Promotes the mascot, but it collides with Piply (a study app) and Pippit (ByteDance's AI content tool).

PilaRejected

Italian and Spanish for "battery" and "stack", but the OECD runs PILA, an education assessment platform. The pronunciation is also unclear in English.

Revo / RevvoRejected

Taken, and part of the crowded "Rev-" group.

Sparko, BitsyRejected

Too close to Sparx and BBC Bitesize, two of the biggest names in UK schools.
