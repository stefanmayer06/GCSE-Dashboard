The name

Clever, joined to Volta at the v

Clevolta is a new word, so no one else owns it. It overlaps two words the way Pinterest overlaps pin and interest: clever and volta share the v. A student reads "clever" first, so the name leads with learning, and all of Volta is still inside it.

{{ICON_CLEVER}}

Clever

The learning half. Clever is what revision is for, and it is a word every 15-year-old already uses. It comes first, so the name reads as learning before energy.

{{ICON_PILE}}

Volt and the pile

Volt lime is the app's one reward colour. In 1800 Alessandro Volta made the first battery by stacking discs, and emblems also stack a layer for each marked success.

{{ICON_TURN}}

The turn

In a sonnet the volta is the turning point, where the argument changes direction. GCSE English students learn the term, and a fixed mistake is the same kind of turning point.

{{ICON_AGAIN}}

Una volta

In Italian, ancora una volta means "one more time". Mistakes come back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days, and each return is one more go.

Coined, with a story

Clevolta

Duolingo and Memrise show the pattern: a new word built from parts that mean something. It tells a story, as Seneca does, and no one else can own it.

What that means for the brand

Stay away from the bolt

Save My Exams' logo is a lightning bolt and Seneca's hero uses ⚡. Clevolta already has volt in its name, so the mark uses the quadrant disc instead.

Sell the loop, not the bank

Free papers and questions are everywhere (Maths Genie, Corbettmaths, PMT, Bitesize). The only story here that competitors can't copy by adding content is the mistake-to-mastery loop, and the closing disc shows it.

Be the game, not the library

Seneca, Save My Exams and Medly all open on white with flat blue or black, and none of them leads with a character. Their gamification is a feature, not the brand. Clevolta can make it the brand, with Pip and the night stage.
